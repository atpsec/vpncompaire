A VPN does not make you invisible online. It protects specific layers, mainly the network path between your device and the VPN server.

## What changes when the VPN is on?

Destinations normally see the VPN server's public IP rather than your home or mobile IP. People on the same local network also have far less visibility into your traffic when the tunnel is configured correctly.

## What can your ISP see?

Your ISP can usually see that you are exchanging encrypted traffic with a VPN server. With proper DNS and IPv6 handling, it should not directly see the destination traffic inside the tunnel.

## Can websites still identify you?

Yes. Logged-in accounts, cookies, browser fingerprints, language, time zone and payment data can all link activity to you. Changing the IP does not reset those identifiers.

## What a VPN does not stop

A VPN does not remove malware, prevent you from typing credentials into a phishing page, or secure an already compromised account. Use passkeys or strong MFA, keep devices updated and review active sessions.

## Build a useful threat model

Ask four questions:

1. Who are you protecting against?
2. Which data matters most?
3. Which apps and devices are in scope?
4. Which controls beyond VPN are required?

## Bottom line

Use a VPN as one layer in a wider security model: VPN + HTTPS + updates + phishing-resistant authentication + sensible browser privacy controls.
