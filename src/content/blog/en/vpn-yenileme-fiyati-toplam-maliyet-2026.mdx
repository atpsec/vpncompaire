The price shown on a VPN landing page is often an introductory rate for the first term. A fair comparison should include the renewal price and the length of each billing period.

## Compare total cost, not monthly equivalent

A “$2 per month” label may require paying two or three years upfront. Record the actual amount charged today and the amount expected at renewal.

## Add the hidden variables

Taxes, currency conversion, optional dedicated IPs, password managers and antivirus bundles can change the final cost. Refund policies also matter because they reduce or increase purchase risk.

## A simple comparison model

For each provider, note:

- First payment
- Number of months covered
- Renewal payment
- Renewal interval
- Tax treatment
- Add-on fees
- Money-back window

Then calculate the cost over the time horizon you actually expect to use the service.

## Avoid one-number rankings

The cheapest first term is not always the lowest long-term cost. Likewise, a higher-priced provider may include features you would otherwise buy separately.

## Bottom line

In 2026, renewal transparency is a core part of VPN value. Compare the full subscription lifecycle instead of the promotional monthly equivalent.
