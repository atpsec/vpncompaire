Opera VPN is easy to misunderstand because the name can describe two different products: the free VPN built into the Opera browser and the paid VPN Pro service. They do not protect the same traffic. The free feature is a browser-level privacy tool; VPN Pro is intended to cover the device.

This distinction is the most important part of an honest Opera VPN review. A browser VPN can be useful for a quick change of apparent IP address inside one browser, but it should not be presented as a replacement for a full VPN application. The information below was checked on August 28, 2026. Features, locations, pricing and availability can change, so confirm current details on Opera's official pages before relying on them.

## Quick verdict

Opera's free browser VPN is a convenient option for people who already use Opera and want a no-subscription browser feature. Opera describes it as unlimited, with no login required, and says it is integrated into the browser rather than installed as an extension. The provider also says that the free tier uses general locations and is designed to protect traffic from the Opera browser.

The limitation is equally important: browser-only protection does not cover other browsers, desktop applications, games, operating-system services or devices that are not using Opera. If you need whole-device coverage, many locations, a kill switch across applications or a provider profile with a broader set of independently verifiable controls, compare full VPN apps instead.

## What is Opera's free VPN?

Opera's free VPN is a proxy-style feature inside the Opera browser. When it is enabled, websites opened through Opera can see the VPN service's network address instead of the visitor's ordinary public address. Opera's help documentation also discusses browser-level behavior around DNS and WebRTC.

That does not mean that every connection from the computer is protected. An app outside Opera continues to use its own network path unless a separate device-wide VPN is active. Opera's own explanation of the feature is therefore more useful than a broad label such as “secure VPN”: it tells us to treat this as protection for browser traffic, with the exact interface and availability depending on the platform, country and browser version.

For a simple demonstration of what a website can observe, you can also use our [internet snapshot tool](/tools/what-websites-can-see). It shows the public network signal and browser/device signals visible to that page; it is not an anonymity score and does not test Opera's entire network.

## Free VPN versus VPN Pro

The following comparison reflects Opera's published product descriptions rather than a VPN Advisor laboratory test:

| | Opera Free VPN | Opera VPN Pro |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Protection scope | Opera browser traffic | Device-wide VPN coverage |
| Cost model | Free browser feature | Paid subscription |
| Locations described by Opera | Three general locations | 48 unique locations |
| Network size described by Opera | 100+ servers | 3,000+ servers |
| Devices | The Opera browser | Up to six supported Windows, macOS and Android devices |
| Best understood as | A convenient browser privacy layer | A conventional whole-device VPN service |

Opera's official page presents the free tier as having unlimited bandwidth, while its help documentation notes that real-world speed still depends on factors such as server load. “Unlimited” should therefore not be read as a promise of a particular speed, latency or streaming result.

The numbers above are provider-published and may change. They should not be compared with a third-party measurement unless the measurement uses the same date, locations, protocols and test conditions.

## What evidence exists for privacy claims?

Opera says that Deloitte reviewed the no-log design and implementation of its free browser VPN infrastructure. Opera's published summary says the review covered desktop and mobile infrastructure, configuration and deployment, relevant documentation and privacy materials, and staff interviews during a 2024 review period.

This is useful evidence, but it needs to be labelled accurately. The source is Opera's summary of a provider-commissioned review, and it describes a defined scope and point in time. It is not a permanent guarantee that every future version, service component or operational practice will remain unchanged. It is also not the same thing as VPN Advisor running a live laboratory test.

Opera's [privacy statement](https://www.opera.com/privacy) should be read alongside the VPN materials. A no-log claim for a VPN feature does not automatically answer every question about browser telemetry, account services, crash reporting, advertising technology or other products. Those are separate parts of the privacy model.

## What Opera VPN can and cannot protect

The free feature can be a reasonable fit when the goal is narrow and clear:

- reducing the exposure of an ordinary public IP address to sites opened in Opera;
- adding a browser-level privacy layer on an unfamiliar Wi-Fi network;
- using a simple free feature without installing a separate VPN client;
- checking how a website responds when the browser's apparent network location changes.

It cannot, by itself, protect traffic from every application on the device. It also cannot stop phishing, malware, weak passwords, account-level tracking, browser fingerprinting or a website from recognizing a logged-in account. A VPN changes one part of the connection path; it does not turn an unsafe site or compromised device into a safe one.

For a broader security plan, combine the network decision with multi-factor authentication, software updates, a password manager and careful handling of suspicious links. ## Who may benefit from Opera's free VPN?

Opera users who want a low-friction browser feature are the clearest audience. It can also make sense for someone who understands that only Opera traffic is in scope and wants a quick, free option for routine browsing. The browser integration is the main convenience advantage.

Look elsewhere if you need protection for email and other apps, a router, a smart TV, gaming traffic, torrent clients or an entire family of devices. You should also compare full VPN providers if a specific country, protocol choice, kill-switch behavior, independent testing scope or support policy is central to your decision. Our [free versus paid VPN guide](/blog/free-vs-paid-vpn) explains why “free” and “full coverage” are not interchangeable terms.

## How to enable it safely

The exact menu can change between Opera releases and platforms. In general, open Opera's privacy or settings area, enable the VPN feature, and use the VPN indicator in the address bar to review its state and location. If the option is missing, check Opera's current help page for platform and country availability rather than installing an unverified extension that uses the Opera name.

After enabling it, verify the scope with a simple test: open a site in Opera, then check an unrelated app or a second browser. A changed address in Opera does not prove that the other application is using the same path. Avoid sharing a full IP address in screenshots or public posts.

## Bottom line

Opera VPN is worth including in a global VPN reference guide because it represents a common browser-integrated model. Its strongest point is convenience: the free feature is already inside Opera and can provide browser-level network privacy without a separate subscription. Its defining weakness is scope: it is not the same as a device-wide VPN application.

We would treat Opera Free VPN as a useful browser privacy option, not as a universal “best VPN” answer. Opera VPN Pro is the closer comparison to full VPN products, but it should still be evaluated using current price, platform, location and privacy documentation.
