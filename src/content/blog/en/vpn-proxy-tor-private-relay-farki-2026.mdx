VPNs, proxies, Tor and iCloud Private Relay are often grouped together, but they solve different problems. The right choice starts with one question: **which traffic are you trying to hide, and from whom?**

## What each tool does

- **VPN:** routes device or selected app traffic through an encrypted tunnel and exposes the VPN server's IP to destinations.
- **Proxy:** usually reroutes one application or protocol; encryption is not guaranteed.
- **Tor:** sends traffic through multiple relays to separate source and destination, with a performance cost.
- **Private Relay:** Apple's Safari-focused privacy layer that uses two relays so no single party sees both your identity and browsing destination.

## When a VPN makes sense

A VPN is the broadest option when you want coverage across many apps, safer use of untrusted Wi-Fi, consistent DNS handling and a different public IP. Features such as a kill switch, IPv6 support and leak protection matter more than marketing claims.

A VPN does not make you anonymous by itself. Logged-in accounts, cookies and browser fingerprints can still identify you.

## When Tor or a proxy may be better

A proxy can be practical for testing one application from another IP. Tor is more appropriate for users who prioritize anonymity and are willing to accept slower speeds and more site friction.

## Private Relay is not a full VPN

Apple describes Private Relay as protecting Safari browsing by encrypting traffic and sending it through two separate relays. It does not provide general full-device VPN behavior and availability varies by region.

## 2026 quick comparison

| Need | Starting point |
|---|---|
| Whole-device traffic protection | VPN |
| One app through another IP | Proxy |
| Strong anonymity model | Tor |
| Safari privacy on Apple devices | Private Relay |
| Low-latency gaming | VPN or direct connection |

## Bottom line

These tools are complementary, not interchangeable. A VPN is the most general-purpose network privacy layer; Tor targets stronger anonymity; Private Relay focuses on Safari privacy.
