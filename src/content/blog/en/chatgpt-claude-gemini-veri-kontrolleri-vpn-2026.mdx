ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini each have their own account, retention and data-control settings. A VPN changes the network path; it does not override the privacy settings of the AI service itself.

## What a VPN changes

The service will generally see the VPN server's public IP instead of your normal IP. Your local network or ISP sees an encrypted connection to the VPN provider rather than the full destination mix.

## What remains account-linked

If you are signed in, the provider still knows which account is using the service. Conversation history, uploaded files, connected apps and product telemetry are governed by that provider's settings and policies.

## Review the controls

In 2026, users should check whether a service offers temporary chats, model-training opt-outs, retention controls, enterprise privacy terms or connected-app permissions. These settings can matter more than IP masking for sensitive prompts.

## Practical guidance

- Do not paste secrets or API keys into prompts.
- Use enterprise or business plans when organizational policy requires stronger contractual controls.
- Review connected apps and revoke unused permissions.
- Use a VPN on untrusted networks.
- Keep account MFA or passkeys enabled.

## Bottom line

AI privacy is a combination of network security and service-level data governance. A VPN helps with the first layer but cannot replace the second.
