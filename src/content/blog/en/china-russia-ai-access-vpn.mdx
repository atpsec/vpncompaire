China's Great Firewall (GFW) and Russia's Roskomnadzor regulations heavily restrict direct access to popular AI tools such as OpenAI ChatGPT, Anthropic Claude, Google Gemini, and Perplexity. Both countries detect and block VPN traffic with different techniques: China uses Deep Packet Inspection (DPI) and protocol fingerprinting, while Russia relies on IP blocklists and the TSPU censorship infrastructure. This guide covers VPN requirements, obfuscation techniques, and practical setup steps for users trying to reach AI tools from either country.

## How China's Great Firewall Works

The Great Firewall is one of the world's most advanced national censorship systems. It operates on three main technical layers:

**1. IP blocklisting**: Known server IP blocks of OpenAI, Google, and Anthropic are blocked outright. Public commercial VPN provider IP pools are also widely identified.

**2. DNS poisoning**: DNS queries for chatgpt.com, claude.ai, gemini.google.com return manipulated, fake IPs.

**3. Deep Packet Inspection (DPI)**: Even encrypted traffic is examined at the metadata level. Standard OpenVPN and WireGuard packets have recognizable fingerprints; the traffic is encrypted, but flagged as "VPN traffic" and dropped.

Since 2022 the GFW has been reinforced with machine-learning traffic classifiers. Vanilla protocols (plain OpenVPN, plain WireGuard) are now identified within minutes.

## Roskomnadzor and TSPU in Russia

Russia's approach is different from China's but similarly restrictive. The TSPU (Technical Means for Counteracting Threats) infrastructure has been deployed at all major ISPs since 2024. ChatGPT, Claude, and other Western AI services are periodically blocked outright; the blocks come in short but recurring waves.

Russia also requires "registration of commercial VPNs in the RKN registry." Unregistered VPN providers are blacklisted and their websites blocked. In practice foreign VPN services keep working, but downloading the apps becomes complicated.

## Why Obfuscation Is Essential for DPI Resistance

In both China and Russia the key concept is **obfuscation** — making VPN traffic look like ordinary HTTPS traffic. Common methods:

### OpenVPN over TCP 443

Carries VPN traffic over the standard HTTPS port as TCP. Not enough on its own, but a baseline layer. Pro: available in every VPN. Con: modern DPI systems can still distinguish it.

### Stunnel / Obfsproxy

Wraps VPN traffic with an extra TLS layer. From the outside, the traffic looks like HTTPS to a web server. NordVPN's "Obfuscated Servers" feature uses this approach.

### ShadowSocks and V2Ray

Not strictly VPNs; these are SOCKS5-based proxy protocols widely used inside China for years. Their traffic profile looks much more "natural," and they have a high success rate against GFW detection. They are among the most-used methods for AI tool access from China.

### WireGuard tunneling (AmneziaWG, Cloak)

Modern projects that preserve WireGuard's speed while hiding its fingerprint. AmneziaWG in particular is an open-source variant designed for the Russian scenario. Our [WireGuard vs OpenVPN comparison](/en/blog/wireguard-vs-openvpn-comparison) covers the basics of both protocols; in highly restrictive environments like China and Russia, neither is sufficient without an obfuscation layer.

## ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Perplexity: Which Is Most Reachable?

Major AI tools do not face the same restriction map. Current state in China and Russia:

- **ChatGPT (OpenAI)**: Direct access blocked in both countries. OpenAI does not offer official service in China or Russia, so without a VPN even login is impossible.
- **Claude (Anthropic)**: Anthropic's API and web app are geo-blocked in both countries. The block hits at signup time.
- **Gemini (Google)**: All of Google's ecosystem is blocked in China. In Russia, Google AI Studio is restricted; the Gemini web app is sometimes reachable.
- **Perplexity**: As a smaller service it sometimes flies under the radar; direct access is occasionally possible from both countries but inconsistent.

The access map changes constantly — what is open today can close tomorrow. So do not rely on a single tool, and keep multiple VPN profiles ready.

## Setup Steps: China Scenario

1. **Buy your VPN subscription before traveling**: Once you are in China, most VPN websites are blocked. Activate your subscription, install the app, and verify the account before crossing the border.
2. **Use obfuscated servers**: Enable your provider's "stealth," "obfuscated," or "camouflage" mode. Hong Kong, Taiwan, and Japan are the closest low-latency server locations.
3. **Multiple protocols ready**: WireGuard for everyday use, OpenVPN over TCP 443 as backup, ShadowSocks/V2Ray for periods of strict blocking.
4. **DNS over HTTPS (DoH)**: Before the VPN tunnel comes up, set system DNS to Cloudflare 1.1.1.1 or Google 8.8.8.8 over DoH so DNS queries are not poisoned.
5. **Kill switch is mandatory**: When the tunnel drops, your real IP must not leak. This is critical in China.

## Setup Steps: Russia Scenario

1. **App download**: Some VPNs are removed from the App Store and Google Play in Russia. You may need a direct APK or an alternative store.
2. **Payment**: Foreign payments with Russian bank cards are difficult. Crypto, gift cards, or foreign cards are common workarounds.
3. **Server location**: Helsinki, Stockholm, and Warsaw offer low latency on EU infrastructure.
4. **AmneziaWG / Cloak protocol**: Use an obfuscated WireGuard variant rather than the vanilla one. Open-source options exist, but built-in support in commercial VPNs is preferable.
5. **Backup access**: Tor + obfs4 bridges can act as a fallback layer for AI tools — slower, but useful for emergencies.

## Legal Dimension

Personal VPN use in China is a grey area — not legal, but everyday users are rarely prosecuted. Foreigners usually face no issues; commercial VPN sales and corporate use are tightly enforced. In Russia, unauthorized VPN use has been an administrative offense since 2024, but again, prosecution of individuals is rare. For details see our [is VPN legal by country article](/en/blog/is-vpn-legal).

To avoid risk on the AI service side, also read its terms. ChatGPT and Claude can object to "access from a region where you are not authorized," but account suspensions usually trigger when abuse is detected, not from geography alone.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Can I reach ChatGPT from China with a free VPN?**
Practically no. Free VPNs do not offer obfuscation, and their IP pools are already on GFW blocklists. Stable access requires an obfuscation-capable commercial VPN; alternatives are listed on our [comparison page](/en/karsilastir).

**Can I use Claude API from Russia?**
Anthropic blocks Russian IPs at the API level too. Access works through European or US VPN exits, but account verification may require a foreign phone number.

**Is WireGuard alone enough in China?**
No. Vanilla WireGuard fingerprints are easily detected by the GFW. AmneziaWG or a WireGuard-over-obfuscation layer is required.

**What if the VPN does not activate after I enter the country?**
You can reach the provider's support site over Tor and request backup protocol info. Some providers email manual configuration files. Still, the best strategy is to have everything ready before entering the country.
