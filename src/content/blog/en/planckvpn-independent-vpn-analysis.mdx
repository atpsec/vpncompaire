**Publishing note:** This is a **source-based analysis**, not a laboratory review. PlanckVPN is **not in the established-provider directory**. We have not run independent speed, streaming, DNS/WebRTC leak or kill-switch tests, and we are **not assigning a score**. The separate PlanckVPN watch card on our [VPN provider guide](/en/vpn-reviews) is outside the Top 10 and does not change the Global Core 30 count.

PlanckVPN is a relatively new VPN positioned around independence, ownership transparency and a zero-log promise. That makes it worth monitoring, but a clear narrative is not the same as independently verified evidence. This article separates the provider's current claims from what VPN Advisor can actually confirm.

## The independence pitch

PlanckVPN's central argument is that VPN ownership deserves more attention. Its public material discusses acquisitions and corporate structures in the VPN industry and presents PlanckVPN as independent, with no parent company. That is a useful question for buyers to ask: who owns the service, where is it incorporated, and what legal entity controls personal data?

The ownership claim is more useful because a person is named on the transparency page and the privacy policy names a legal controller. It is still not the same as an independent corporate audit. We have not independently verified every corporate filing, and readers should treat the provider's own pages as primary statements rather than neutral evidence. For comparison, [Mullvad](/en/reviews/mullvad) and [Proton VPN](/en/reviews/proton-vpn) have a longer public evidence trail.

## Privacy policy: no traffic logs is not no metadata

PlanckVPN's privacy policy says it does not log browsing history, DNS queries, destination addresses, traffic content, per-site bandwidth or destination-tied timestamps. That is the relevant no-activity-logging claim.

The same policy also describes data needed to operate an account and subscription service, including subscriber email, an app-generated device identifier, a WireGuard public key, the last server and connection time within a stated retention window, and billing records. Those fields do not automatically mean the service logs browsing activity, but they do mean that “zero logs” should not be interpreted as “no data of any kind exists.”

There is also a documentation consistency point worth watching: the comparison page lists **Stealth**, while the privacy and transparency pages prominently describe WireGuard, OpenVPN and IKEv2. Until the provider's documentation uses the same protocol inventory, we treat Stealth as a provider-reported feature that needs clarification rather than as a fully verified capability.

## Security evidence and audit gap

PlanckVPN has a [security and responsible-disclosure page](https://planckvpn.com/security), which is a positive sign for how security reports should be handled. But a disclosure policy is not an audit, and it does not establish the security of the apps, infrastructure or no-logs implementation.

PlanckVPN's own [VPN trust checklist](https://planckvpn.com/blog/vpn-trust-checklist) says that no independent audit of its no-logs policy has been completed and that the apps are not open source. This is unusually clear self-disclosure, but the evidence gap remains material for a privacy product. Until an independent audit or reproducible technical evidence is available, we cannot place PlanckVPN alongside providers with a repeated third-party audit record.

## Source checklist (no score)

We applied our [source-based methodology](/en/methodology) using public information only. We did not convert missing evidence into a numerical penalty or invent test results.

### 1. Privacy policy and jurisdiction

- **Positive:** The privacy policy distinguishes activity data from account and device data and names a legal controller.
- **Open question:** Virginia, United States is a relevant jurisdictional fact for users with a high jurisdiction-sensitivity threat model. The legal entity and ownership statements should be kept current.

### 2. Independent audits

- **Material gap:** No completed independent no-logs or security audit is publicly identified. The provider's own checklist acknowledges this.

### 3. Speed and network evidence

- **Unknown:** VPN Advisor has not measured speed, latency, DNS behaviour or Turkish route performance. The provider describes the network as small and growing, but does not publish a complete current server list on the comparison page.

### 4. Streaming compatibility

- **Unknown:** We have no independent Netflix, Disney+, BluTV, Exxen or regional streaming results. A VPN should not be recommended for a specific streaming service without current, reproducible testing.

### 5. Pricing and subscription terms

- **Positive:** The current official pages expose one paid plan at $7.99/month or $59.99/year. There is no current free-tier claim in the pages reviewed.
- **Check before purchase:** Renewal price, taxes, refund conditions and platform-specific billing terms can change; confirm them at checkout.

### 6. Platform coverage and usability

- **Positive:** The comparison page now lists iOS, iPadOS, Apple Silicon Mac, Windows, Android and Linux, with up to four devices.
- **Unknown:** Platform availability on a page is not the same as a hands-on usability or kill-switch result. We have not tested the clients.

## What the current evidence means for users

PlanckVPN may be worth early attention for a user who:

- wants to investigate a provider that presents itself as independent;
- values a named owner and published privacy/transparency pages;
- needs a subscription-only product rather than a free VPN;
- is comfortable with a United States jurisdiction and up to four devices;
- understands that the no-logs claim remains provider-reported.

Waiting is more sensible for a user who:

- requires an independent no-logs or security audit;
- needs VPN Advisor speed, leak or kill-switch measurements;
- needs a long incident history and a mature public evidence trail;
- needs guaranteed streaming access or a documented country/server map;
- is making a high-risk journalism, activism or enterprise decision.

## Why it is visible, but not ranked

PlanckVPN is now easier to find on VPN Advisor through a clearly labelled **emerging provider watch** card and this updated source analysis. That visibility is editorial discovery, not a recommendation. It is intentionally separate from the Top 10 and from the Global Core 30 market directory so that the number of established providers does not imply that PlanckVPN has already met our evidence threshold.

A future detailed provider profile would require several of the following:

1. an independently published no-logs or security audit;
2. a stable, transparent server and country inventory;
3. consistent protocol and privacy documentation;
4. VPN Advisor hands-on testing of speed, DNS/WebRTC leaks and kill-switch behaviour;
5. a longer public operating and incident history.

## Conclusion

PlanckVPN is editorially interesting because it makes ownership and independence part of its product story, and its current public documents are more detailed than the earlier launch-era material suggested. The strongest caveat is unchanged: the no-logs and security claims have not yet been backed by a completed independent audit or VPN Advisor testing.

**Bottom line:** PlanckVPN is a **source-based watch candidate, not a ranked recommendation**. We will reassess it when the provider publishes an independent audit, stabilises its technical documentation and accumulates a longer evidence trail.
