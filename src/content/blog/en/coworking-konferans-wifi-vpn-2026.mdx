Coworking spaces and conferences often provide fast internet, but the network is shared with people and devices you do not control. That makes them a good example of where a VPN is useful but not sufficient.

## Before you connect

Confirm the official SSID, disable automatic joining and update your device. If a captive portal asks for unusual permissions or software installation, verify it with the venue.

## Connect the VPN early

Start the VPN before opening corporate dashboards, source-code repositories or cloud consoles. If your company provides a managed VPN or Zero Trust client, follow that policy instead of adding an unmanaged consumer tunnel on top.

## Protect the account layer

Use passkeys or MFA for email, GitHub, cloud providers and password managers. A VPN cannot protect an account if a session token is stolen or a phishing page captures credentials.

## Reduce exposure

Turn off local file sharing and network discovery. Avoid exposing development services to the local subnet. Use HTTPS and SSH keys for administrative access.

## Quick checklist

- Official SSID confirmed
- VPN or corporate access client connected
- MFA/passkeys enabled
- File sharing disabled
- Device firewall active
- Sensitive work avoided on unknown captive portals

## Bottom line

Coworking and event Wi-Fi can be used safely when network protection, account security and device hardening are treated as separate layers.
