VPN providers offer monthly, yearly, 2-year, and sometimes 3-year plans. At first glance, longer commitments are appealing — the per-month price drops more than half. But what's the catch, what risks come with it, and which term suits whom? This article compares VPN subscription terms on payment, flexibility, refund policy, and long-term risk.

For VPN selection, see our [comparison page](/en/karsilastir) and [VPN selection guide](/en/blog/how-to-choose-vpn).

## Typical Pricing Structure (2026)

Premium VPN providers' common pricing template (NordVPN, Surfshark, ExpressVPN, ProtonVPN examples):

- **Monthly**: $12-15/month
- **1-year**: $4-6/month (paid upfront, 50-60% off)
- **2-year**: $2.5-4/month (paid upfront, 70-80% off)
- **3-year**: $2-3/month (some providers, 80-85% off)

Mullvad uses a different model: flat 5 EUR/month, no term discount. This itself is a privacy stance — minimal user data retention.

## Monthly: Who Should Choose It?

Pros:
- Flexible — cancel any time
- Low commitment — only that month
- Great for testing

Cons:
- Highest per-month price (3-4x more)
- Easy to forget renewal

**For**: One-off travel VPN, short project, undecided use. See our [VPN for travel article](/en/blog/vpn-for-travel) for detail.

## 1-Year: Sweet Spot

Best price-flexibility balance for most users.

Pros:
- 50-60% lower per-month
- Renewal cycle allows adapting to provider changes
- If the provider becomes worse or shuts down, 1 year of loss is manageable

Cons:
- Upfront cost still high
- Per-month price higher than 2-year

**For**: Regular VPN users who want to monitor industry shifts.

## 2-Year: Most Popular

The plan most providers push and most customers pick.

Pros:
- Lowest per-month price ($2-4)
- Big total savings ($200-300 over 2 years vs monthly)
- Locked-in pricing for 24 months

Cons:
- 2 years is long — provider ownership can change, no-logs policy can break
- VPN industry saw frequent ownership shifts in 2024-2025
- $50-100 upfront, hard to recover if you lose access

**For**: Users who've tracked the provider, trust the brand, prioritize cost optimization.

## 3-Year and Beyond

Some providers (older Surfshark deals, occasional campaigns) offer 3-year or even "lifetime" plans.

**Warning**: Be cautious of "lifetime" VPN deals. "Lifetime" is the provider's, not yours. If the company shuts or sells, your investment can vanish. Our [free vs paid VPN article](/en/blog/free-vs-paid-vpn) covers these risks in depth.

3-year plans carry even higher ownership-shift risk. As the industry matures, consolidation is inevitable.

## Ownership Changes and No-logs Risk

Recent VPN industry examples:
- Popular free VPNs acquired by ad-data aggregator companies
- Brand name unchanged but infrastructure quietly migrated
- "No-logs" policy modified under new ownership

Provider selection isn't just today's quality — it's future ownership stability. Therefore:

1. Prefer providers with independent audits
2. RAM-only server architecture ([RAM-only servers article](/en/blog/ram-only-servers-vpn)) makes log retention technically impossible
3. Question ownership transparency

## Refund Policy

Most premium VPNs offer a 30-day money-back guarantee. This is critical to subscription term selection:

- Buy the 2-year plan, test for 30 days, full refund if you don't like it
- Risk-free window to test long-term commitment

Refund policy gotchas:
- Some providers cap bandwidth (e.g., over 7 GB used = no refund)
- Refund time depends on payment method (card 3-5 days, crypto often non-refundable)

## Renewal Trap

Cheap-first-term, expensive-renewal pattern is widespread:
- 2-year plan bought at $100
- After 24 months, auto-renews at $200 for the same plan

Fix:
1. Set a reminder 1 month before renewal
2. Cancel and re-sign with the new-customer discount
3. Switching providers is also valid — the market is dynamic

## Regional Pricing Advantage?

Some users connect to India or Turkey IP and try to subscribe. Results vary:
- Most VPN providers use single-currency pricing
- Some shifted to PPP (purchasing power parity)
- Your card's country largely determines price — connecting via VPN with a Turkish card doesn't change it

This strategy works better for digital services: [ChatGPT Plus savings](/en/blog/chatgpt-plus-pricing-vpn-savings), [Steam regional pricing](/en/blog/steam-regional-pricing-vpn), [Spotify](/en/blog/spotify-regional-pricing-vpn).
