Travel eSIMs make it easy to get mobile data in another country, but they change the connectivity provider, not the entire privacy model. A VPN can still be useful for work and account access while travelling.

## eSIM versus VPN

An eSIM provides cellular connectivity. The carrier or eSIM partner may still observe network metadata. A VPN creates an encrypted tunnel over that connection and changes the public IP seen by destinations.

## Why routing can be unusual

Some travel eSIMs route data through another country. That can affect latency, geolocation and service behavior even before a VPN is enabled. Check the observed IP location instead of assuming it matches your physical location.

## Public Wi-Fi still matters

Digital nomads often switch between cellular data, hotels, cafés and coworking networks. A VPN is especially useful when moving onto networks you do not manage.

## Work checklist

- Keep a backup eSIM or physical SIM plan.
- Use passkeys or MFA on work accounts.
- Test the VPN before travel.
- Enable a kill switch if your workflow requires it.
- Verify DNS and IPv6 after connecting.
- Keep recovery codes offline.

## Bottom line

An eSIM solves connectivity; a VPN solves a different network-security problem. For long-term travel, combine both with strong account security and device updates.
