Netflix buffers, YouTube won't offer 4K, game ping spikes — but a speed test looks fine. That pattern often matches ISP throttling: your provider deliberately limits certain traffic types or busy hours. VPN encryption can hide traffic type and sometimes bypass those limits — not always.

## What Is ISP Throttling?

Throttling is when an ISP applies lower bandwidth to specific apps or protocols. Common targets:

- **Streaming** (Netflix, YouTube, Twitch)
- **Gaming** and peer-to-peer traffic
- **Torrenting**
- **Peak-hour** slowdowns (evening congestion)

Users often report "only YouTube is slow" or "internet speeds up at night". Official policies vary by provider; transparency can be limited.

## How VPN Affects Throttling

On VPN, the ISP typically sees encrypted traffic to a VPN server, not packet contents. If limits target **traffic type** (e.g. video CDN), VPN may mask it and improve speed.

Our [VPN speed guide](/blog/vpn-speed-optimization) covers protocol and server choice.

VPN **won't help** when:

- The whole line is congested
- The ISP throttles VPN traffic specifically
- Wi-Fi or line quality is the real issue
- You picked a distant or overloaded VPN server

## How to Detect Throttling

1. **Speed test vs real use** — fast test, slow Netflix: classic sign.
2. **VPN on/off comparison** — same time, same content.
3. **Try protocols** — WireGuard often beats OpenVPN; see [protocol comparison](/blog/vpn-protocols-comparison).
4. **Try nearby servers** — geography matters.
