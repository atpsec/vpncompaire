Almost every privacy-focused VPN uses the phrase “no logs.” The phrase alone does not tell you what telemetry is collected, how long operational data is retained or what happens when authorities request information.

## Start with the privacy policy

Look for specific categories: connection timestamps, source IP addresses, assigned VPN IPs, DNS queries, bandwidth counters, crash telemetry and payment data. A strong policy defines both what is collected and what is not.

## Read the audit scope

An independent audit is useful only if you understand what was tested. Check the date, auditor, systems included, limitations and whether the final report or a detailed summary is public.

## Transparency reports

A transparency report can show how many legal requests, copyright notices or other demands a provider received. It is most useful when published on a regular schedule with consistent categories.

## Infrastructure claims

RAM-only servers, diskless designs and reproducible configurations can reduce persistence, but architecture does not automatically prove a no-logs claim. Operational controls and account systems matter too.

## 2026 evaluation checklist

1. Is the privacy policy specific?
2. Is the audit recent enough to be relevant?
3. Can you see the audit scope?
4. Does the provider publish transparency information?
5. Is ownership and jurisdiction clear?
6. Are major incidents disclosed publicly?

## Bottom line

Trust should be built from verifiable evidence, not a marketing phrase. The best providers make it easier to inspect policies, audits, architecture and legal-request history.
